LFTDO — Validity

CQ-L1 — Logical Consistency

The ontology demonstrates strong logical consistency, supported by Pellet reasoning and a large validated ABox. The absence of inconsistencies across 19,716 axioms indicates a well-formed TBox–RBox–ABox alignment. However, logical consistency does not inherently confirm semantic completeness or real-world fidelity.

Verdict: Highly consistent and logically sound

CQ-L2 — RBox Reasoning Capability

The 27 SWRL rules cover behavioural, contextual, health, and sabotage scenarios comprehensively. Multi-factor reasoning (e.g., RL_COMPLEXTHEFT_CORRELATION) demonstrates strong inferencing depth. However, temporal reasoning remains implicit rather than formally modelled.

Verdict: Strong reasoning coverage with minor temporal limitations

CQ-L3 — Competency Question Coverage

The SPARQL queries demonstrate that most competency questions can be answered. However, CQ3 and CQ5 rely on implicit temporal reasoning, indicating partial coverage rather than full semantic completeness.

Verdict: Broad CQ coverage with minor gaps

CQ-L4 — Temporal & Spatial Representation

Spatial modelling via geofencing is robust and rule-driven. Temporal reasoning is present through durations and thresholds but lacks explicit temporal ontology constructs.

Verdict: Strong spatial modelling, moderate temporal modelling
Key Gap: Lack of explicit temporal ontology (TimeInterval, EventWindow) limits advanced forensic reasoning.

Overall Verdict:
The Onto-AIoTA architecture and LFTDO ontology together form a highly coherent and innovative DSR artefact pair. The solution demonstrates strong validity, high-quality ontology engineering, and practical utility within rural livestock farming contexts. The semantic reasoning capability significantly enhances theft detection efficacy beyond traditional systems.
