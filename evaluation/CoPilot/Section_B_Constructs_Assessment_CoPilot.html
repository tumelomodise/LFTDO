B.9.1 · LFTDO Constructs Assessment

Vocabulary & Domain Coverage
BQ‑L1 Are the core LFTDO constructs clearly and unambiguously defined?

The foundational and theft-detection constructs (Livestock, Behaviour, BehaviourState, TheftIndicator, TheftEvent) are defined with sufficient semantic precision and explicit domain grounding. Clear subclassing (NormalState vs AbnormalState) and explicit purpose statements reduce terminological overlap and ambiguity.

5
Strongly Supported
BQ‑L2 Does the vocabulary cover all concepts required for livestock theft scenarios?

The ontology explicitly models health, environmental, spatial, behavioural, and technological dimensions. Constructs such as HealthCondition and EnvironmentalCondition strengthen contextual reasoning and reduce false positives.

5
Strongly Supported
BQ‑L3 Are stakeholder roles clearly distinguished?

Participant subclasses (Farmer, LawEnforcementAgent, Veterinarian, UnauthorisedPerson) are well differentiated. The UnauthorisedPerson construct adequately models theft intent without conflating legitimate actors.

4
Supported
BQ‑L4 Is the NormalState / AbnormalState distinction precise?

The distinction is conceptually sound and operationalised via baseline values and deviation thresholds, enabling SWRL-based inference. Explicit numeric ranges further strengthen automation readiness.

4
Supported
Structural Relationships & Internal Coherence
BQ‑L5 Does the detection chain form a coherent pathway?

The semantic chain from Livestock through BehaviourState to TheftEvent is logically complete and internally consistent. Each object property represents a necessary reasoning transition.

5
Strongly Supported
BQ‑L6 Are contextual disambiguation relationships sufficient?

Health and environmental influence properties significantly reduce misclassification risk. Additional confidence weighting could further enhance inference robustness.

4
Supported
Requirement & Theme Alignment
BQ‑L7 Do LFTDO constructs fulfil R1 Semantic Interoperability?

The unified vocabulary for heterogeneous sensor data is a core strength of LFTDO. SensorData abstraction supports consistent semantic interpretation.

5
Strongly Supported
BQ‑L8 Does the detection-to-notification chain support R3 and R4?

The linkage from TheftEvent through DetectionMethod and SoftwareApplication to Participant validation explicitly supports real-time reasoning and governance.

5
Strongly Supported
BQ‑L9 Do CQ1–CQ8 test all critical knowledge boundaries?

The competency questions cover identity, spatial, behavioural, temporal, and governance reasoning. Longitudinal adoption metrics are not explicitly tested.

Introduce competency questions addressing system adoption and long-term effectiveness.
3
Partially Supported
BQ‑L10 Is the vocabulary accessible to non-technical stakeholders?

Core concepts are intuitive and domain-aligned, though ontology-internal terminology may require interface-level abstraction.

4
Supported
Construct Design Quality
BQ‑L11 Are SensorData and BehaviourState properties sufficient?

The defined data properties support threshold-based reasoning. Explicit uncertainty modelling would further strengthen inference reliability.

4
Supported
BQ‑L12 Does LFTDO align with established livestock ontologies?

The taxonomy aligns conceptually with ATOL, EOL, and AHOL while justifiably extending them for theft detection specificity.
